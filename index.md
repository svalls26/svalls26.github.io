  <h1 class="bio__eyebrow">About me</h1>
  <p>I recently completed the MPhil in Machine Learning and Machine Intelligence at the University of Cambridge. For my dissertation, I created a human–AI collaboration system to help reconstruct manuscripts from the <a href="https://www.cam.ac.uk/stories/discarded-history-treasures-of-the-cairo-genizah">Cairo Genizah</a>. Developed through a participatory design approach with scholars, the system uses a computer vision pipeline to retrieve potential joins based on visual similarity. The project was selected for the <a href="https://www.ai.cam.ac.uk/news/from-safer-workplaces-to-medieval-manuscripts-five-new-ai-deas-projects-put-ai-to-work-on-real-world-problems/">ai@cam AI-deas Sprint</a> to develop it into a production-ready system for practical use.</p>
  <p>Looking ahead, I aim to continue doing research on human-centred machine learning. My interests span how AI can support complex, context dependent tasks, leveraging human expertise to improve everyday workflows, and how models can be adapted through interaction data. I am now seeking PhD opportunities that bring AI closer to expert domains, improving their decision making and creating meaningful impact across history, science and society.</p>
  <p>Previously, I worked as a data engineer at startups and big tech, and lived in Switzerland, Spain and Luxembourg. I also played competitive chess during high school, winning several regional championships and reaching a rating of 2000. You can check out my “immortal game” <a href="#immortal-game" aria-label="Replay my immortal chess game">here</a>.</p>
