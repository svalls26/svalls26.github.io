# Research imagery and historical sources

`solomon-schechter.jpg` is the unmodified photograph “Solomon Schechter at work in Cambridge”, supplied by Cambridge University Library through [Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/solomon-schechter-at-work-in-cambridge-cambridge-university-library/1gFAH8WCN2YE-A). The photograph’s inscription dates it to 1898. It is served locally at 1200 × 1059 pixels. The source credits Cambridge University Library and states: “Reproduced by the kind permission of the Syndics of Cambridge University Library.” That is the source’s rights statement; no new permission or open licence is asserted here.

Historical context for Schechter’s letter and the Ben Sira fragment was checked against Cambridge University Library’s [Genizah Fragments, no. 31](https://wwwe.lib.cam.ac.uk/GF/31/) and the [Lewis-Gibson Genizah Collection](https://www.lib.cam.ac.uk/collections/departments/taylor-schechter-genizah-research-unit/lewis-gibson-genizah-collection).

The research summaries are based on Sara Valls Navarro’s [MPhil dissertation](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1f63JcBByUEnj4Q4bZbJxVZQWwxJD1nya/view?usp=sharing), especially chapters 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6. The full PDF is linked rather than copied into the website repository.

The Join Problem visuals were supplied by Sara: `join-set-example.png`, `seder-anchor.png`, `seder-join.png`, and `expert-workflow-reference.png`. Their file contents are unchanged. The central fragment is T-S A8.3; the plain and ornamented seder-marker close-ups are T-S A9.17 and T-S A6.8. The manuscript images are credited to Cambridge University Library in the dissertation; no new permission or open licence is asserted here. Cue explanations and the marker comparison follow chapter 4, especially table 4.3 and figure 4.6.

The retrieval pipeline follows dissertation Figure 5.7 (printed page 41, PDF page 63), which is also the diagram Sara supplied in “Screenshot 2026-10-25 at 19.26.59.png”. `retrieval-plate.png`, `retrieval-ink.png` and `retrieval-patch-placement.png` are the original 1800 × 1084 panels, extracted losslessly with Poppler; `retrieval-patches.png` is the original 816 × 548 patch panel. The example manuscript is T-S 16.112, Cambridge University Library. The website rebuilds the surrounding labels and connectors as SVG, preserving the writing-unit extraction, frozen DINOv3 ViT-L/16, foreground-token pooling per classmark, VLAD descriptor and ranking stages. The four raster panels are unchanged.

`retrieval-codex-recall.svg` preserves the vector plot from Figure 5.13 (printed page 52, PDF page 74), including the four bars, values, axes and legend. It was extracted with Poppler after setting a crop rectangle in PDF points to (140, 260, 444, 476); no data were reconstructed or re-plotted. The figure shows mean traversal recall on the held-out Phoenix (90 covered members) and Prophets (39 covered members) codices. At k = 10 the values are 0.580 and 0.267; at k = 100 they are 0.978 and 0.900. Section 5.3.2 defines the iterative traversal, and section 5.4.5 reports codex results: start from one member, query each newly discovered recorded partner, and assume that every surfaced recorded partner is recognised. The text retains this assumption and the averaging over seed members.

Dataset figures follow sections 5.1.1–5.1.4 and tables 5.1–5.2: 3,593 join groups, 10,153 fragments and 24,057 images across six collections. The model link points to [Meta’s official DINOv3 page](https://ai.meta.com/research/dinov3/).

`join-discovery-reference.png` is an unchanged copy of Sara’s supplied “Screenshot 2026-10-25 at 16.12.57.png”, showing the same discovery/confirmation visual requested again in “Screenshot 2026-10-25 at 16.35.02.png”.
